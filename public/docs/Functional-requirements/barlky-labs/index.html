02 — The Legal Structure

Barkly Labs should be treated as two separate milestones:

State level

Michigan nonprofit corporation

This creates the legal corporation under Michigan law.

Michigan currently lists the formation filing for a domestic nonprofit corporation as:

Articles of Incorporation — Nonprofit

Form 502

Total filing/franchise fees: $20

Federal level

IRS 501(c)(3) tax-exempt recognition

This is separate from Michigan incorporation.

Barkly Labs should pursue federal recognition under Section 501(c)(3) if the intended mission and activities qualify.

Depending on the organization's circumstances, the IRS may permit Form 1023-EZ or require the full Form 1023. The IRS requires organizations using Form 1023-EZ to complete the eligibility worksheet first.

06 — EIN

Barkly Labs needs its own Employer Identification Number.

The IRS requires a nonprofit's EIN application to identify a responsible party. The responsible party must be a person who controls or exercises effective control over the organization and its funds/assets.

Checklist:

Obtain EIN from IRS

Save IRS confirmation

Do not use the founder's personal SSN as Barkly's EIN

Store EIN documentation securely

09 — $0 MONEY STATE

Barkly Labs may begin with little or no money.

That does NOT mean:

"Barkly owes a giant tax bill because it exists."

Instead, the organization still needs to maintain its legal and reporting obligations.

For a small 501(c)(3), the IRS generally allows organizations that normally have gross receipts of $50,000 or less to use Form 990-N instead of a full Form 990, subject to the applicable rules.

The IRS also warns that failing to file a required annual return/notice for three consecutive years can result in automatic revocation of tax-exempt status.

Therefore:

$0 revenue does not mean $0 paperwork.

But paperwork is not the same thing as owing income tax.

12 — Charitable Solicitation

This is a separate compliance area.

If Barkly asks the public for donations in Michigan, Michigan's Charitable Organizations and Solicitations Act may require registration.

Michigan states that most 501(c)(3) organizations that solicit or receive contributions in Michigan must register, while exemptions exist. One example is organizations that raise funds exclusively through volunteers and receive less than $25,000 per year.

Therefore:

Before launching a major public fundraising campaign:

Determine whether Barkly is exempt

If required, register with Michigan's Attorney General

Maintain solicitation records

Renew when required

Keep donor acknowledgements

Do not assume that "we're a nonprofit" automatically means fundraising registration is unnecessary.
